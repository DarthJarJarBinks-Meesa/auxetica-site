The Biology & Engineering
Why titanium implants fail —
and how geometry fixes it.

Stress shielding is not a material problem. It is a structural one. Auxetica solves it at the architectural level.

01

Cellular Response

Bone healing depends on mechanotransduction: osteocytes sense local strain and signal progenitor cells to differentiate into osteoblasts that build new bone. Studies consistently show that physiologic loading supports osteoblast proliferation and matrix formation, while insufficient loading impairs these pathways.

02

The Stiffness Mismatch Problem

Titanium has an elastic modulus of 110 GPa — between 5× and 550× stiffer than cortical and cancellous bone. When an implant is far stiffer than the surrounding tissue, it absorbs nearly all applied mechanical load, leaving bone mechanically unloaded.

03

Wolff's Law & Stress Shielding

In accordance with Wolff's Law, bone remodels in response to the mechanical forces it experiences. Deprive bone of that stimulus and it undergoes progressive resorption — reducing periprosthetic bone density, degrading the bone-implant interface, and increasing fracture risk.

Finite element analyses of solid titanium femoral implants have quantified up to 56% stress shielding in the proximal femur — meaning more than half of all physiological load bypasses the bone entirely.

04

Negative Poisson's Ratio

Conventional materials expand laterally when compressed and contract under tension. Auxetic structures do the opposite — they contract laterally under compression and expand under tension. This behavior emerges entirely from unit cell geometry, with no modification to the base material required.

05

Restoring Mechanotransduction

By replacing bulk titanium with an auxetic lattice, the deformation behavior of the structure enables more natural, distributed load transfer to the surrounding bone tissue. FEA of auxetic titanium hip implants demonstrates a reduction of periprosthetic stress shielding from 56% to 18% in optimized auxetic designs.

Mechanobiology of Healing
Bone cells need mechanical stimulus to heal effectively.

Bone regeneration is not only biochemical; it is mechano-biological. Osteocytes and skeletal progenitor cells respond to local strain by activating osteogenic pathways that increase osteoblast differentiation, proliferation, and extracellular matrix production. When this mechanical signaling falls below physiologic levels, osteogenesis is blunted and remodeling shifts toward weaker repair.

Conventional high-stiffness implants can shield surrounding bone from normal load transfer. That unloading reduces the mechanotransductive signals required for healthy differentiation and proliferation, which can contribute to slower healing, lower periprosthetic bone quality, and higher long-term failure risk. Auxetic load-sharing architectures are designed to preserve this stimulus and support stronger biological integration over time.
